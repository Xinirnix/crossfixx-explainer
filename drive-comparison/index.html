What the equations say

Backlash on reversal

A nut needs clearance on its thread, and a worm needs clearance between its teeth. When the screw reverses, it turns through that clearance b before the other flank touches, so the payload stops while the motor keeps going: the chart shows a step of size b at every reversal. A load that always pulls one way keeps the payload on one flank and hides the gap, which is what anti-backlash nuts and preloaded ball nuts do on purpose. The piezo tip pushes the payload directly by friction, "with no screw and no gearing" (Xeryon), so there is no flank to cross; what remains is the encoder step, 78 nm on the XLA.

Heat while holding

An electromagnet only pushes while current flows: F = kf·I, and that current heats the winding as P = I²R = R·(F/kf)². A linear motor holding a load therefore "draws current the whole time it holds position" (Xeryon). A stepper keeps its phases energised to hold its step, P = 2·I²R whatever the load. A self-locking worm needs no motor torque to hold, so its motor can rest. The piezo motor "draws no current to stay put" (Xeryon): its preload spring does the holding.

Stray magnetic field

A magnet track with pole pitch τ has a field that falls off as B(z) ≈ B₀·e^(−πz/τ). Close to the track it is hundreds of millitesla; the payload rides right above it, so "it carries a magnetic field into your instrument" (Xeryon). A motor housing at the end of a screw behaves roughly like a dipole, B ∝ 1/r³, and is further away. The piezo drive is ceramic, with no coils and no magnets, so the drive itself adds no field. Earth's field, about 0.05 mT, is shown for scale.

Self-locking at zero power

A screw or worm with lead angle λ = atan(lead / πd) cannot be turned backwards by its load when μ > tan λ. A plain lead screw usually meets this; a ball screw (μ ≈ 0.005) does not and "needs a brake" (Xeryon), and a worm with more starts has a steeper lead and stops locking. A linear motor has nothing to hold it: with the power off, a vertical load falls. The piezo motor "self-locks at rest with a holding force equal to its driving force" (Xeryon), here Fhold = μ·Fpreload. Push past it and the contact slips; Xeryon notes this is "passive holding within the specified holding force, not a safety brake".

Sources: Xeryon, Precision Motion Technologies Compared (holding at power-off, current while holding, magnetic field, 400 / 1.4 / 2000 mm/s speeds, 78 nm XLA encoder) and CrossFixx piezo motor (166 kHz, ~1 µm tip motion). Xeryon's comparison does not give backlash figures, worm gear data or field strengths, so those use textbook relations with example values.
