Why an open-loop piezo misses its target

hysteresis · creep

A piezo stack grows when you put a voltage on it, but not along a straight line. Its position depends on whether it got to that voltage from above or from below. That is hysteresis, typically 10 to 15 % of the commanded motion. And after the voltage stops changing, the stack keeps moving a little, more slowly over time. That is creep, about 1 % of the last move for every tenfold increase in elapsed time.

Both come from the ceramic itself (PI's tutorial names "crystalline polarization effects and molecular friction"), and neither shows up in the voltage. So if you position open loop, setting a voltage from a straight-line calibration, you can be off by several percent of the stroke. A position sensor in a closed loop removes both, because it measures where the stage really is.

From PI's tutorial

  • Hysteresis "is typically on the order of 10 to 15 % of the commanded motion". The displacement "depends on whether it was previously operated at a higher or a lower voltage".
  • The tutorial shows the loops "for various peak voltages": lower peak voltage, smaller loop.
  • Creep after a voltage change: ΔL(t) ≈ ΔL0.1 · (1 + γ · lg(t / 0.1 s)), where ΔL0.1 is the displacement 0.1 s after the change is complete and γ, the creep factor, is "on the order of 0.01 to 0.02". That is about 1 % of the last move per time decade, and "a few %" after a few hours.
  • "Creep only occurs with open loop PZTs", and closed-loop actuators eliminate hysteresis.
  • For periodic motion neither hurts repeatability: the stack comes back to the same point on the same loop.

Our model (editable in src/hysteresis-creep/model.js)

  • Assumed: a 0–100 V stack with 30 µm nominal stroke and a 36 mm body. Replace these with your actuator's datasheet values. PI's own example is a 50 µm actuator.
  • Assumed shape: PI gives the loop's width but not its exact shape. We use a Prandtl–Ishlinskii model: a straight line plus 24 "play" (backlash) elements of different widths. It gives nested loops that grow with the peak voltage, as in PI's figure. Its weights are scaled so the full loop is as wide as the hysteresis slider says.
  • Assumed: creep applies the PI formula to every voltage change and adds them up. So a slow drag creeps too, and a step back down creeps back down.
  • Assumed: the closed loop is an ideal sensor plus an integrator that moves the voltage until the position matches. A real controller has limited bandwidth and sensor noise (see the closed-loop page).
  • Not modelled: temperature, ageing (PI calls it negligible for actuators) and the dynamic response of the stack.
