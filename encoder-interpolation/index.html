How an optical encoder reads nanometres from a 20 µm grating

encoder · interpolation

The read head shines light on a scale with a fine grating. As the scale moves, the photodiodes see the brightness pattern slide past and turn it into two signals, a sine and a cosine, 90° apart. One full wave of both is one grating period: 20 µm on SmarAct's METIRIO scale.

Plot sine against cosine and the point goes round a circle, once per period. Its direction says which way the stage moves. Its angle says where inside the period the head is: x = φ / 2π · 20 µm. Split the circle into 4096 counts and one count is 4.9 nm. That is interpolation.

It only works if the circle really is a circle centred on zero. An offset shifts it, a gain mismatch squashes it, a phase error tilts it. The angle the electronics read is then wrong by an amount that repeats every period: the cyclic error.

Why the error repeats once or twice per period

Offset

The circle's centre moves, but the electronics still measure the angle from zero. On the side the circle moved towards, the angle reads too small; on the far side, too big. One bump per turn: error ≈ P/2π · o, about 32 nm for a 1 % offset on a 20 µm period.

Gain mismatch

One signal is bigger than the other, so the circle becomes an ellipse along an axis. The angle is pulled towards the long axis twice per turn: error ≈ P/2π · g/2, again about 32 nm for a 2 % mismatch.

Phase error

The signals are not exactly 90° apart, so the ellipse is tilted 45°. Also twice per turn: error ≈ P/2π · δ/2, about 56 nm for 2°. It adds a constant shift too, which homing hides.

Correction

The interpolator records the Lissajous figure, fits an ellipse, and maps it back onto a circle before taking the angle (Heydemann's method). Here the fit is perfect, so the error drops to the count step. Noise and higher harmonics are what is left in practice.

From the sources

  • SmarAct: an array of photodiodes turns the moving brightness pattern into "analog sine and cosine signals", 90° apart, "a so-called quadrature signal". One sine alone can't tell direction.
  • SmarAct: plotted against each other they form a Lissajous figure, ideally a circle; one turn is one scale period, the turning direction is the direction of motion.
  • SmarAct: "The METIRIO® scale uses a 20 µm pitch", and position within the period is x = φ / 2π · 20 µm. The quality of the figure shows "signal balance, phase accuracy and alignment quality".
  • Xeryon's encoders resolve 1250, 312, 78, 5 or 1 nm per count (Controller & Connection Manual). That count is what the controller closes the loop on.

  • cos = A(1 + g/2)·cos θ + ox, sin = A(1 − g/2)·sin(θ + δ) + oy, read angle = atan2(sin, cos). Standard textbook relations; SmarAct doesn't describe the error terms.
  • The first-order error formulas above are textbook approximations; the plot is computed exactly from atan2.
  • Assumed: the 4 and 40 µm periods, the interpolation factors, every error and noise value on the sliders and presets. Replace them with datasheet values for a real head.
  • Not modelled: higher signal harmonics, contamination patches, the reference mark, interpolator latency.

The controller only sees counts. On the closed-loop page PTOL, the zones and every gain are in these counts, so a cyclic error larger than PTOL means the stage lands "within tolerance" at a place that is off by up to the cyclic error, and lands differently in every period.
